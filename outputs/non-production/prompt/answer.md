I have completed the planned exploration of patterns and trade-offs in performance, price, vehicle condition, and ownership costs using Cedar Lane Motors' inventory and vehicle-history data.

Data Summary:

- Inventory has 240 vehicles, mostly Used (210) with some New (30). Average price is about $35,076, average mileage about 18,661 miles, average horsepower about 276.
- Historical dataset covers 48 model-year entries with average reliability around 7.9/10, owner satisfaction 7.8/10, and average annual repair cost $558.
- Inventory segments include Midsize SUV, Adventure SUV, Compact Sedan, Electric Hatchback and others.
- Powertrains include Gasoline, Electric, Hybrid, and Plug-in Hybrid.

Key Findings:

1. Performance vs Price (Inventory Scatter Plot):
The scatter plot of horsepower versus price shows a general positive correlation: higher horsepower vehicles tend to have higher prices. However, there is variation within horsepower ranges reflecting differences in segment and condition. This aligns with performance-handling principles where more horsepower can translate to sportier or more powerful driving, often costing more. But price is not determined by horsepower alone.

2. Price by Segment (Inventory Bar Chart):
Average prices vary notably by segment. Luxury SUVs and Adventure SUVs command higher average prices, while Compact Sedans and Electric Hatchbacks trend lower. This reflects different market positioning, buyer expectations, and performance profiles—larger SUVs prioritize space and presence, while compact cars emphasize efficiency and urban use.

3. Repair Costs by Segment (History Bar Chart):
Segments with higher average repair costs historically include Adventure SUVs and Luxury SUVs. Lower repair costs appear in segments like Compact SUVs and Electric Hatchbacks. Ownership cost trade-offs emerge, as high-performance or premium vehicles may have increased maintenance expenses.

4. Condition Impact on Price:
A statistical test comparing New vs Used inventory prices confirms New vehicles average significantly higher prices (around $45,389 vs $33,603). Buyers pay a premium for new condition, which also often pairs with full warranty, lower mileage, and peak performance.

Limitations and Follow-ups:

- Correlations observed do not prove causation. Vehicle condition, brand, features, and market factors also affect prices and ownership costs.
- The historical dataset is smaller and model-year aggregated, limiting granular matching with current inventory.
- Performance perception involves more than horsepower—handling, weight, and suspension tuning also influence sporty appeal but aren't directly captured here.
- Follow-up could analyze how AWD, seating capacity, or specific brands affect price and performance trade-offs.
- An integrated cost of ownership analysis combining repair, insurance, fuel, and resale could better inform economic trade-offs.

In summary, evidence shows clear trade-offs in price, performance potential (horsepower), and ownership costs by vehicle segment and condition. Using performance-handling insights, higher horsepower and new condition vehicles ask for price premiums that reflect sporty and premium driving appeals, balanced against ownership expenses revealed in history data.
