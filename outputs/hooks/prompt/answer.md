Analysis Results:

1. Dataset Overview:
- Inventory dataset comprises 240 vehicles with a price range from $14,128 to $60,661 and horsepower ranging from 178 to 390.
- Vehicle condition in the inventory is mostly "Used" (210 vehicles) with 30 marked as "New."
- Vehicle history dataset includes 48 records spanning various model years, brands, and powertrains.

2. Descriptive Statistics:
- Average inventory price is about $35,076, with a wide spread across the range.
- Average inventory horsepower is approximately 276.
- Historical average annual repair costs across vehicles average around $558.

3. Performance vs. Price Pattern (Scatter Plot):
- There is a positive correlation of 0.71 between horsepower and price in the dealership's inventory, showing higher horsepower vehicles tend to be priced higher.
- Horsepower ranges broadly associate with prices, indicating buyers pay more for performance capability.

4. Ownership Cost by Powertrain (Bar Chart):
- Average annual repair costs vary by powertrain type in historical data:
   - Plug-in Hybrids have the highest average repair cost (~$674).
   - Gasoline vehicles follow (~$607).
   - Electric vehicles show lower average repair costs (~$530).
   - Hybrids have the lowest average repair costs (~$457).

Findings and Trade-offs:
- There is a clear trade-off between performance (horsepower) and price: more powerful vehicles are generally more expensive.
- Vehicle condition in inventory is predominantly used, which might impact price and expected ownership costs.
- Ownership repair costs tend to be higher for plug-in hybrids and gasoline vehicles compared to electrics and hybrids, which could affect total cost of ownership.
- Buyers valuing performance need to consider higher upfront prices and possibly higher repair costs depending on powertrain.
- The inventory diversity offers choices across condition, performance, and powertrain types, supporting varied buyer preferences.

Limitations:
- Correlations between horsepower and price do not prove causation; other factors like brand, model, and features can influence pricing.
- Repair cost averages are historical and aggregated by powertrain type, not precise predictions for any specific vehicle.
- The condition measure is categorical and does not detail exact vehicle mileage or damage, which affect ownership cost estimations.
- Sample sizes and dataset coverage limit the generalizability of conclusions to broader markets.

This analysis provides data-supported insights into how performance, price, vehicle condition, and ownership costs relate within Cedar Lane Motors' current inventory and historical vehicle data.
